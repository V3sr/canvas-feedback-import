Feedback CSV format

How to fill in the spreadsheet so grades and written feedback go into Canvas together.

  1. Open your Canvas course and click the extension button.
  2. Get template: tick the lab or assessment you're grading and download. The ones due closest to today are at the top. In a big course, type in the search box (for example "lab 5") or pick an assignment group. "Tick all" selects every match.
  3. Open the file in Excel or Google Sheets. Type grades and feedback as you go.
  4. Save as CSV. In Excel: File > Save As > CSV UTF-8. In Google Sheets: File > Download > CSV.
  5. Upload & import: choose the file, check the preview, and import.

The rules

Blank cellNothing happens. A blank grade never erases a grade, and a blank feedback cell posts nothing.
GradesNumbers like 8 or 8.5 (use a period, not a comma). 85% also works. Complete/incomplete assignments take complete or incomplete. Type EX to excuse a student.
FeedbackAny text. Each cell becomes one comment on that student's submission. For a new line inside a cell, press Alt+Enter (Windows) or Option+Return (Mac).
Keep togetherThe Student and ID columns. Each row's name is checked against its ID, so if rows get shuffled, nothing is posted to the wrong person. Sort the whole sheet, never just one column.
Don't editThe column headers (the numbers in brackets link them to Canvas) and the "Template downloaded" note.
Fine to changeDelete students you're not grading, filter by section, reorder columns.

Excel gotchas

  • Feedback that starts with - or + or = turns into #NAME?. Start with a letter, or type ' first (for example '- check your labels). The tool catches this and won't post it.
  • Scores like 8/10 can turn into dates (8-Oct). Type just 8.
  • Decimals are saved the way they're displayed. If a column shows no decimals, 8.5 saves as 9. Widen the format before saving.
  • If Excel asks about keeping the CSV format when you save, choose Keep / Use CSV.

Good to know

  • Nothing is saved until you confirm. The preview shows each grade change (old → new) and each comment. Anything that looks off is unticked for you to check.
  • Uploading the same file twice is safe. Comments already on a submission are skipped, and matching grades are left alone.
  • If you already left a comment: choose Add a new comment to keep both (handy when a TA and an instructor both give feedback), or Replace your last comment to fix a mistake. Replacing only ever changes your own most recent comment, and the preview shows the old text crossed out above the new one.
  • Imported the wrong thing? Use Undo this import on the results screen, or later from the Upload tab. Grades go back to what they were, comments the import added are removed, and replaced comments get their earlier text back. Anything someone changed in Canvas since is left alone. Only the most recent import can be undone.
  • Importing isn't the same as posting grades. For assignments set to post grades manually, students see nothing until you post grades in the Gradebook.
  • Newer grades are protected. If someone changes a grade in Canvas after you downloaded the template, that grade isn't overwritten. The preview tells you which ones.
  • Who sees it: if the assignment posts grades automatically, students see new grades and comments right away and may get a notification. If grades are posted manually, they see them when you post grades.
  • Comments show your name. They're posted from whoever is signed in to Canvas.
  • Group assignments graded as a group: comments are posted to each student, but group grades should be set in SpeedGrader.
  • Rubrics and quizzes: grades set here skip the rubric, and a quiz may re-score itself. The template marks these.
  • Not supported: anonymous or moderated grading, unpublished assignments, and closed grading periods.
  • Several TAs? Each TA can download a template for just their section and upload it separately.

Grading on paper? Fill in the sheet afterwards and upload it once. Grading on an iPad? Use the Google Sheets app, then download as CSV on a computer.
